title: Two nights, same sky
question: Our two images show the same sky on two nights. What is different between them, and how can you tell?
terms: [observing conditions, real change, seeing, PSF]
---

Two pictures of the same sky can differ for two kinds of reason. Something in
the sky really changed: a [[real change]]. Or the [[observing conditions]]
changed: where the telescope pointed, how bright the background was, the
exposure time, and the [[seeing]], which sets how blurred each star looks.

For this pair the exposure time is the same on both nights. We call night 1
the [[reference image]] and night 2 the [[target image]].

:::slot two-nights
title: The two nights
caption: Same colour scale. You can ask for zooms, crops, profiles or pixel values.
:::

:::your-turn what-changed
title: What changed?
prompt: For each possible difference (pointing, background, blur, a source that changed): did it happen here, and what's your evidence?
:::

:::closure
found: Some differences are real changes; the rest come from observing conditions, and those must be removed before comparing.
raises: Can we simply subtract the reference from the target and see only the real changes?
:::
