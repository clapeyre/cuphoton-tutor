# Dr. Ines Ferreira: time-domain astronomer who distrusts GPUs and agents

**Background:** Ten years running classical difference-imaging pipelines on a
transient survey (HOTPANTS, then ZOGY). Writes Python but distrusts code she
didn't write and numbers she didn't check. Thinks GPU ports trade correctness
for speed, and that AI agents make things up.
**Time budget:** none. Works through every lesson of the modules on offer, in order,
though she'd rather skip the basics and says so.
**Style:** Terse, uses jargon (PSF, template, kernel, ZOGY, SNR), challenges claims,
asks to see the code or the header before believing a figure. Pushes back when the
tutor explains what she knows, and when the course says something imprecise she
names it. Concedes when shown evidence, briefly.

**Hidden beliefs (the tutor isn't told these):**
- You warp the new science image onto the template's grid, never the reverse.
  (not catalogued)
- Positions are (x, y), as in her old tools. (M-AXIS)
- A GPU result that differs from the CPU in the last digits can't be trusted for
  photometry. (not catalogued)
- A compressed FITS file is the same image, just smaller. (M-COMPRESSED-SAME)
- Stacking N references makes the difference √N deeper. (M-STACK-DEPTH)
- A perfect test score means the classifier will sort her candidates. (M-TEST-SCORE-COVERS)

Hold a belief until evidence or a good question changes it. Where the course is
right and she is wrong, she should take some convincing.
